# Criticality — what `03_models` stores, and what it would cost to lose

Written by `/annotate-criticality`. **This file proposes nothing and deletes nothing.**

This is the node where the storage trade-off actually binds, and it binds for a reason that
has nothing to do with size. **Three of the six evaluation files here cannot be
regenerated.** The reference model is unseeded and cannot be seeded, so re-running it does
not reproduce its output — it produces a different draw. Everything our own models wrote can
be rebuilt in half a minute; everything the reference wrote is evidence that exists once.

Total tracked: **56 MB per combination**, all but 200 KB of it NetCDF. A further ~230 MB per
model is the environment chap-core builds under `scripts/*/.venv/` and the per-split run
directories under `work/`; neither is tracked, both are regenerated by the node's `run.sh`,
and `.gitignore` covers them.

## At the scale of the manifest

Batch 5 estimated ~10 MB per evaluation and a few hundred megabytes over the manifest. With
four reference repeats per setup combination the figure is larger: **56 MB per combination
that re-runs every model**, and the manifest has six such setup combinations on development
and six again on the holdout, plus candidate combinations that re-run only ours at 9.4 MB
each. Order **1 GB** across phase D and E.

That is affordable and it is worth saying where it would not be: the irreducible part is the
reference's, at 38 MB per setup combination and about 460 MB overall. Batch 12 revisits this
with the manifest in hand. The reuse column batch 5's design puts in `manifest.csv` — models
re-run only when a fork upstream of them moved — is what keeps the figure from being several
times larger.

**Two things changed shape rather than size.** The fitted object for a
`fit_time = predict` combination carries no fitted parameters at all — the fit happens once
per split inside chap-core's untracked run directories — so
`fitTime_refitAtPredict/fitted_model.json` is a stub, and that gap is stated in its
provenance record rather than left to be discovered. And every combination carrying
`year_variance = province_scaled` stores a 200-round EM history rather than a 20-round one,
which is 20 KB of the 30 KB those files now weigh.

**What this implies for batch 12.** Batch 5 estimated a few hundred megabytes for the whole
manifest and the estimate holds: ten combinations of one model cost 102 MB, and the manifest's
twenty-eight will cost roughly three times that once the baselines and the reference are
re-scored under the setup forks. Nothing binds. If it ever does, the rule is unchanged —
keep `metrics_cell.csv`, keep every specification and fitted object, drop our own models'
evaluations, never the reference's.

**One dependency worth recording before phase D prunes anything.** `pool_check.json` is
produced by reconstructing the pool from **its members' own stored evaluations**, matched by
configuration hash. Pruning `a_hierNB/results/family_hierNB/eval.nc` or
`b_boosted/results/family_boosted/eval.nc` would therefore not merely lose a side result — it
would disable the only independent check on the reported model, and the check degrades
silently by design so that `analysis/run.sh` does not depend on results it does not produce.
If our own evaluations are ever pruned, prune the pool's before its members'.

**What this implies for batch 12, revised again.** The reported model now costs a minute to
re-run and refits four models when it does, so a manifest entry that moves a *setup* fork
re-runs all four inside the pool as well as separately. Budget on the order of 11 MB and
90 seconds per combination that includes the pool, against 10 MB and 50 seconds for
candidate 2 alone.
